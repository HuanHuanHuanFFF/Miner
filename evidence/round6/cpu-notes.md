## Previous experiments constrain interpretation

`ROUND4.md` and `evidence/round4/cpu-notes.md` record CPU experiments on the fast3/#432 family, not these H functions:

- Disabling fast3 read-ahead regressed total time by about 1.78%; outlining its main `run1` regressed about 4.32% and lost constant specialization. Neither mechanism is repeated here.
- `run1t` inlining and zero-flush had small initial improvements that did not independently reproduce. `classoutline`, delayed second loads, slot-only and related source rewrites did not establish a stable CPU gain.
- Same-process order reversal changed the sign of several small effects. A source-level reduction, single runner, or favorable block is insufficient for adopting these variants.
- H8 core trimming reduced proof cost, but it was not an algorithm speedup. Later H16/depth/mode experiments change search/refit policy and are not CPU evidence for these attribute-only variants.

The fixed candidate release profile is already `opt-level=3`, fat LTO, one codegen unit and overflow checks enabled (`sources/conjectures-optimisation-deflate/validator/measure/candidate/Cargo.toml`). There is therefore no assumption that the compiler currently lacks inlining. The [Rust Reference](https://doc.rust-lang.org/reference/attributes/codegen.html#the-inline-attribute) describes inline modes as code-generation hints; the compiler may ignore them, and poor inlining choices can hurt performance. This is why the planned same-binary assembly diagnostic is part of interpreting a result, not an optional proof of benefit added afterward.
