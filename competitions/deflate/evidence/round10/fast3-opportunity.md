# Fast3 opportunity audit

This read-only audit finds no fast3-family probe worth dispatching on current evidence. Round 10 should keep its balanced-frontier work as the main route. A speed-side fast3 successor would only add same-hotkey payment if it displaced #453 from the official frontier; the current task context reports snapshot 27460 with #453 still on-frontier and payable weight `0.0034836620532421396`, so a new point receives no additional same-hotkey share while #453 survives. Admission, registration ownership, bounty eligibility, and any future award remain **UNKNOWN**.

`r3-432-fast3` is a public #432 derivative: `T_SKIP` changes 4→3 and `P_DEPTH` changes 4→1. Its Rust source SHA-256 is `bae8014121e4710f4a34ce63452578b4bf69121b4f695e1b1356780a019108ef`; its `Parse.lean` SHA-256 is `e2c200cf084eca95eb70d26c0efb04e70d88f015315610d2a54d20b6adb7cf04`, byte-identical to the base proof per the manifest. Round 03 records a fresh official extraction and gate for this derivative. It is inherited public work, not a new algorithm.

I recomputed the four raw blocks at `evidence/round3/37493517227/round{1,2,3,4}-r3-432-fast3.jsonl`. Each has the same 28 input filenames and per-file input hashes, 11 measured repetitions after warmup, and no recorded method errors. Fast3 token and output hashes match across all four blocks for each filename. Their equal-file time axes are 0.431623, 0.433063, 0.432552, and 0.432236; the equal-block mean is **0.432368**. The equal-file output-size axis is **36.602139%** in each block. The JSON receipt includes exact raw-byte SHA-256 values and calculation details.

For component attribution, I took each file-block's median parser `time_s` and encoder `encode_s` over the 11 measured repetitions, computed each component's share of their sum, then gave all 112 file-block observations equal weight. This yields **16.39% parser** and **83.61% encoder**. These are diagnostic component shares; median `total_s` is the score input and need not equal the sum of the two component medians.

The deliberately optimistic sensitivity sets parser time to zero while keeping each measured encoder time fixed. Dividing that encoder median by the same file-block's incumbent median total time gives a modeled time-axis floor of **0.373545**, a **13.60%** reduction from the observed 0.432368 axis. This is a counterfactual ceiling, not an attainable gain, experiment result, or statistical bound. It also shows why another parser-only source cleanup has limited reach while the official encoder accounts for most component time.

The prior search does not leave a credible, distinct probe that meets the requested bar. More aggressive fast2 settings were both slower and larger. NICE variants did not show stable time gains; nearby parameter candidates dmin257 and tshift3 missed the fixed screening threshold. Round 04 already tried output-preserving CPU rewrites: disabling read-ahead and forcing `run1` out of line were slower; run1tai's small gain on one runner reversed on another; foldni washed out; later CPU/layout variants were slower or failed to reproduce small gains. Lazy/cost variants also did not produce a stable total-time improvement without a size tradeoff. The measurement sensitivity does not turn those past failures into evidence for a new candidate.

I recommend **zero new fast3 probes**. No untried, low-proof-cost change is presently supported as likely to improve total time at equal or smaller output. That is a judgment from this bounded audit, not proof that no such optimization exists. A future independently evidenced speed-side point would still need to dominate #453 geometrically and pass the official eligibility checks before it could replace that same-hotkey position. This audit did not perform those actions.
