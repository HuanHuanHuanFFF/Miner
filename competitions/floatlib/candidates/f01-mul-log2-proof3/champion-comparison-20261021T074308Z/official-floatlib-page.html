Optimisation · Float kernels

Faster float kernels, proved exact.

Miners submit one Lean file with four binary32 kernels: FloatLib's add and mul, and TorchLean's sum and batched matrix multiply built from them. Each one is proved equal to its specification, bit for bit. An entry 3% faster than the champion becomes the new champion, and the last three champions share the payout 60/30/10.

How to enter

One file, one hotkey, one try per registration

An entry is a single Kernel.lean sent from the miner client in the competition repository. Run the same gate on your machine first, against the shipped reference, before you spend a registration.

  1. 1Register a hotkey

    Register on Subnet 66. Each registration allows one submission, and every stored entry spends it, whatever the outcome.

  2. 2Set up the kit

    On Linux x86_64, clone the competition repository and run ./setup.sh. It installs Lean, FloatLib, Mathlib, the sandbox and the miner client, and can be rerun after an interruption.

  3. 3Measure, then prove

    Start from the shipped reference. gate.py --bench times an entry before its proofs are done; the full gate checks policy, proofs and axioms, then times it.

  4. 4Submit

    Send Kernel.lean, at most 256,000 bytes, signed with your hotkey. The same file twice from one hotkey is refused.

How it is scored

The rules

  1. 01The competition sets 20% of the validator's weights. The rest goes to the treasury.
  2. 02The last three champions share it: 60% to the current one, 30% and 10% to the two before it. A place nobody holds yet goes to the treasury, and so does everything while the shipped champion holds.
  3. 03Every entry is timed against the current champion: its total time over the champion's across twelve input types, three per kernel. Lower is faster, and a faster add or mul counts again inside sum and bmm.
  4. 04An entry takes over at 0.970× or lower, at least 3% faster, and only if its gain holds at the 5th percentile of a bootstrap over its 25 paired rounds (2000 draws). A copy of the champion never takes over.
  5. 05After a takeover the board starts again: every later entry is timed against the new champion.
  6. 06Every output must match the champion's bit for bit, including NaN, infinities and subnormals. One mismatch rejects the entry.
  7. 07Every stored entry spends its registration, whatever the verdict, including a validator error. Only a refusal before the entry is stored is free.
  8. 08If the champion cannot build or finish its own bench, its board pauses. Waiting entries keep their place, cost nothing and are retried. Only an operator can demote a champion.

A proof outlives the network that paid for it.

Whatever becomes of this subnet, a conjecture settled here stays settled: in the record, readable, and rerunnable by anyone who doubts it.
