## A: interval theorem and machine obligations

Let n>0, n<=2^L, 0<=L<=20, m_i in [0,2^48), I_i<=H and s_i in {-1,+1}. Put B=H+48+L-62 and q_i=trunc_zero(s_i*m_i*2^(I_i-B)), T=sum q_i. Then:

1. |q_i|<2^(62-L). For I_i>=B this is exact left shifting; otherwise it is truncation after a right shift. Applying the sign after unsigned truncation is necessary to implement truncation toward zero.
2. Every prefix satisfies |sum_prefix q_i|<2^62. Use the sum of absolute values and prefix length<=n, not only a final-total bound. Thus each signed64 addition is exact; the product itself fits 48 bits. n<=2^20 additionally makes T-n and T+n signed64-safe.
3. Each discarded error has magnitude <2^B before the common 2^-298 scaling, so with a=2^(B-298)>0 the exact finite dot S satisfies (T-n)*a < S < (T+n)*a. The closed interval is a safe conservative enclosure.
4. A shift count >=64 must take a separate branch. For right shifts of a 48-bit product, count>=48 already implies quotient zero. The count is an integer until its sign/range has been proved; do not cast a negative count to Nat or UInt and hope the runtime matches the algebra. For left shifts, use the magnitude bound before the UInt64 operation.

In fact the allowed left-shift count is at most 14-L<=14, since I_i-B<=H-B=14-L. A separate branch for zero product can simplify implementation, but no large left shift is needed for this parameterization.

For the conservative initial theorem, add H+L<=377. Since B-298=H+L-312<=65, |T|<2^62 and n<=2^20<2^61 give |(T+-n)*a|<3*2^126. This is below M=(2-2^-23)*2^127, the greatest finite binary32 value. The true sum is also in [-M,M]. All three dyadic rounds are therefore finite. The guard applies to the endpoints as well as the true sum; bounding only the true sum would leave the direct monotonicity proof incomplete.

**Conditional interval theorem.** Let lo<=S<=hi be exact dyadic values, with all three rounded results finite. Suppose the exact endpoint rounders produce the identical word w, and `(w & 0x7fffffff) != 0`. Their rounded real values agree. By monotonicity, roundAt(lo)<=roundAt(S)<=roundAt(hi), hence all three real values coincide. Nonzero finite injectivity then makes the actual middle rounded Model equal to the endpoint Model. Applying toUInt32/using the word rounder agreement yields the same raw word.

The phrase “nonzero raw word” should be replaced by the magnitude-bit guard above: raw `w != 0` also accepts negative zero. This is a **proof-interface clarification**, not by itself a counterexample to full endpoint-word equality. A separate signed-zero proof could allow more cases, but the present nonzero-injectivity theorem cannot handle them. Exact zero depends on the original dot's zero provenance; ordinary real equality deliberately forgets it. Cancellation, all-zero inputs, empty input and all special operands stay on the original path. Bias is counted as an exact term before certification.

**Fused ReLU extension, independently checked after the initial pass.** The actual `ContractExact.lean` definition `reluW` maps every negative non-NaN word to raw +0; this includes -0 and -Inf. If all dot operands and bias are finite, their exact sum is a real S, never NaN. If S<=U<=0, its RNE word is negative finite, -0, +0 or -Inf. Therefore `act true (roundDot S)=0`. In the interval construction, the cheap sign condition is T+n<=0 because the common scale is positive. This branch does not need the H+L finiteness guard: negative overflow is also erased by the existing ReLU. It still needs the exact interval and accumulator bounds. The empty-dot/all-zero/cancellation signed-zero convention is immaterial after ReLU. `dotOf` constructs same-length arrays, and the unequal-length error is not involved. This theorem is limited to the actual fused `relu=true` output, with bias already inside S. Moving it before a later bias, normalization or residual addition is invalid. For example S=-1 followed by bias +2 has final ReLU 1, whereas dropping S to 0 early produces 2. The general postmap endpoint argument requires monotonicity and a codomain where the retained value determines the output; no broader formalization is proposed here.

Inf is not covered by the real-valued bridge. The overflow threshold is O=M+2^103=2^128-2^103. Positive exact values >=O round to +Inf; negative values <=-O round to -Inf, including equality. The max-finite significand is odd, while the virtual next power-of-two significand is even. Thus infinity cells are convex too, but need a separate direct threshold lemma. The conservative H+L guard avoids this obligation initially. NaN never follows from finite exact real arithmetic and must never be certified via a `toReal` equality.

### Complete paper boundary theorem, separate from the conservative library bridge

The actual truncation bound is strict: lo<S<hi. Under this stronger premise, endpoint raw-word equality suffices for the complete output word even when the common endpoint word is signed zero or infinity. This does **not** claim that the existing real-valued Lean bridge already proves it.

For a positive nonzero finite binary32 word, its RNE cell is bounded by the midpoints to the previous and next representable values; include a midpoint precisely when its significand is even. For the greatest finite word, use the virtual successor 2^128, so the upper threshold O is excluded. The negative cell is its reflection. These cells are convex. The +Inf/-Inf cells are [O,infinity) and (-infinity,-O]. On nonzero exact real arguments, the +0 cell is (0,2^-150] and the -0 cell is [-2^-150,0); both include the half-minimum-subnormal tie because zero is even.

If lo<0<hi, exact endpoint rounders have different sign bits, including underflow and overflow, so their raw words cannot agree. Consequently, an accepted strict interval can never have S=0. If the common endpoint word is +0, lo>=0 and strictness gives S>0; if it is -0, hi<=0 and strictness gives S<0. In both cases the true dot has the same underflow sign without using its exact-zero provenance flags. A zero endpoint can use either zero sign: an endpoint choice that disagrees with its nonzero interior side simply prevents equality. All other outputs follow from the convex cells above. Thus strict interval + raw equality + finite exact operands is a paper-complete certificate, including ±0 and ±Inf, with no extra numeric range guard.

Strictness cannot silently be weakened while dropping the zero guard. For the merely closed interval lo=S=0, hi=2^-150, canonical endpoint rounding gives +0 at both ends, but a source dot consisting only of a negative-zero product can have actual output -0. This is a counterexample to a generic closed-enclosure/raw-equality theorem with unspecified exact-zero provenance. It is not reachable under A's strict positive-radius truncation hypotheses. An optimization that computes a zero residual radius would have to restore a zero-provenance branch or an appropriate output guard.

For a future implementation, choose explicitly between the conservative finite/nonzero theorem with already-inspected library lemmas and this fuller word-cell theorem. The latter may improve coverage but has additional formalization obligations. No scope expansion or candidate change follows from this paper result.

## B: cell theorem, width guards and exponent transport

For P>0, D>0 and positive normal candidate z with field Z and significand M in [2^23,2^24), write z=M*2^(Z-150). For exact x=(P/D)*2^E let k=E+151-Z and construct exact positive A,B so A/B=(P/D)*2^k: A=P*2^k,B=D for k>=0; A=P,B=D*2^-k otherwise. Set T=2BM.

For Z>=2, upper endpoint condition is A<T+B, or equality with M even. For M>2^23 the lower condition is A>T-B, or equality with M even. For M=2^23 the predecessor has half the successor spacing, so the lower condition is 2A>2T-B, or equality with M even. These exactly describe the RNE cell. Multiplication by the positive scale preserves inequalities. Neighbor parity alternates, and M is the stored integer significand, so the tie ownership is exactly the stated parity rule. Negative values follow by sign symmetry; mathematical zero is separately canonical +0 in stable normalization.

For the RMS specialization E=0, 103<=Z<=152, 0<P<2^48 and 2^23<=D<2^24, k is in [-1,48]. If k>=0, prove P*2^k<2^64 **before** using the shifted A (the round-trip shift guard is valid only with the explicit count range). If k=-1 then B=2D<2^25. In all cases BM<2^49 and T+B<2^50. Test the upper bound first; when it passes, A<2^50 and 2A<2^51, so the power-of-two lower comparison cannot overflow. These facts hold even for arbitrarily inaccurate proposal words. No semantic assumption about native proposal generation is required for certificate soundness.

For general Nat N,D and E the same mathematical lemma holds, but the word-width bounds above do **not** transfer automatically. Keep a pure integer theorem separate from machine lowering. In particular a 24-bit denominator bound for RMS does not prove a LayerNorm numerator/denominator representation has the same width.

**Exponent transport:** in the present interface the source has Z>=2 (indeed RMS uses 103..152). If RNE(x)=z and exponent translation leaves z normal (target field 1..254), returning the exponent-translated word is sound. For target fields 2..254 its entire rounding cell is precisely the scaled source cell, including max-finite's open upper endpoint. At target minimum normal with M=2^23, the target cell expands on the lower side because subnormal predecessor spacing equals successor spacing. The scaled source cell is still a subset, so this special case is sound too, although conservatively rejecting it is acceptable. This is a sufficient implication, not equality of all source/target cells. When the translated result is subnormal, the implication fails in general; use the exact final-value cell checker or fallback. The source-range hypothesis must not be dropped: a source word equal to minimum normal already has the enlarged lower cell, which may not remain inside a later ordinary power-of-two cell after scaling upward. The initial review's surrounding Z>=2 context is now explicit in this theorem statement.

The normal-domain guard is checked **before another rounded scaling operation**, from the source raw exponent plus exact exponent adjustment. Checking only that a subsequent native-scaled output happens to be normal is insufficient: a double-rounded result can cross from the correct largest subnormal into the smallest normal. The explicit witness below distinguishes these two guard positions. It does not invalidate normal exponent transport or the newer direct normalized-quotient certificate.

The stable denominator paper bound is valid: 2^-149<=q<=M^2+M gives 2^-75<sqrt(q)<M+1/2<O. Rounding cannot go below representable 2^-75 or above M, so the raw field is 52..254. The layer variance bound requires the exact mean identity and n>0; pointwise |x_i-mean|<=M would be false, and is not the argument being used. No compiler proof or normalization cost reduction follows merely from this bound.

**B's subsequent normalized integer-quotient realization.** In `RMS-QUOTIENT-CERTIFICATE.md`, normal nonzero x/g gives P in [2^46,2^48), and the shared denominator D is in [2^23,2^24). The two comparisons choose h=22,23,24 so `(A,B)=(2P,D),(P,D),(P,2D)` has 2^23<=A/B<2^24. For arbitrary integer q, the exact inequalities qB<=A<(q+1)B imply q=floor(A/B). Then r=A-qB is in [0,B), and `q + [2r>B or (2r=B and q odd)]` is RNE of A/B. The exponent formula Ebase=ex+eg-ed+h-23 follows by substituting the three exact binary32 scalings. Guard 1<=Ebase<=253 and pack `(Ebase-1)*2^23+m`: m=2^24 carries into Ebase+1<=254; m>=2^23 rules out signed-zero ambiguity. q<=2^24 gives (q+1)B<2^50 and 2r<2^26. A +/-1 correction must re-establish this quotient guard before products; in UInt64 arithmetic q=0 followed by q-1 wraps, so reusing only the pre-correction guard is invalid. Alternatively check q>0 before decrement. This review adds no benchmark sample or implementation.

**B's broader high-word/sticky form, same cell lemma.** Let x=(N/D)*2^E with N>=0,D>0 and j=E+152-Z. Write N*2^j=H+f with H=floor(N*2^j),0<=f<1 and sticky iff f>0. This is the quantity 2A when the denominator remains D. Put T=2DM, U=2T+2D, and lower L=2T-2D normally or L=2T-D when M=2^23. The exact upper condition is `H<U or (H=U and not sticky and even M)`; the exact lower is `H>L or (H=L and (sticky or even M))`. Their equivalence follows by the integer separation between H and each integer bound. With 0<D<2^38 and M<=2^24-1, U=2D(2M+1)<2^64; the lower bounds are positive and smaller. This sharper product bound is necessary to justify the addition after doubling T. H<2^64 must be checked before converting H to UInt64. Negative expressions first use their absolute numerator and apply the final sign; a signed arithmetic shift of N is not the stated positive-floor/sticky operation. This is a new paper realization reviewed once, not another randomized test batch.
