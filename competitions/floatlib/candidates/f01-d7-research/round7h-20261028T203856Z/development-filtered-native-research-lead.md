# R24 independent high-ceiling mathematical lead

Status: INFERRED research proposal, no new-target implementation/CI. Current target clarification pending; common deadline unchanged. Do not treat this as a correct algorithm or a measured speedup.

Primary leads: [Saiki et al., When Double Rounding is Correct, 2026 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09005), [author design note](https://www.bsaiki.com/blog/2025/11/14/numbers-libraries.html), [Boldo/Melquiond rounding-to-odd](https://guillaume.melquiond.fr/doc/05-imacs17_1-article.pdf). The new paper mechanizes format-containment conditions in Lean4; this does not prove an arbitrary repeated binary64 reduction equals a round-once binary32 exact dot. No performance numbers from these papers are transferred to the contest.

Different structural route from integer-limb tuning: finite binary32 products have at most48 significant product bits, so binary64 product can be exact within its exponent range; repeated binary64 summation is generally rounded, not exact. A fast filter could accumulate in binary64, derive a rigorous conservative absolute error enclosure, and return the common binary32 rounding of both endpoints only when the entire enclosure belongs to one rounding cell. Every ambiguous/special/guard-failing case falls back to the exact retained algorithm, including cancellation and negative-zero-only rules. This pays small native arithmetic plus one certificate instead of exact limb/carry/round work on every product.

First falsifiers and unknowns: compare full former2 algorithm to ensure this is absent; prove product decode equivalence and summation enclosure, including no binary64 overflow/underflow on the selected finite/n-bounded domain; directed endpoint construction must remain outward without changing hardware rounding modes. A repeated RNE sum is not round-to-odd and cannot borrow that theorem. Bias is another exact term before final rounding; finite guards and valid raw getD behavior must be preserved universally. A bound based on maximal product exponent and n can avoid accumulating sumAbs but may be too loose under cancellation. Actual certificate success coverage, branch cost, word/F64 decode overhead and compiler ABI are UNKNOWN until a real full-lifecycle diagnostic is authorized and run.

A simpler independent discriminator is a common dyadic-lattice/exponent-span certificate under which all native binary64 partial sums are exact. That may be cheap to prove but could have narrow input coverage once activations have full24-bit mantissas; first test reachability rather than assuming ML values are short integers. Stable normalization needs its own exact-statistics and output-rounding enclosure; a dot speedup does not automatically enter the stable-domain implementation.
