# Independent Jam17 runtime cost boundary

One read-only cost review to support the selected arithmetic route, not another arithmetic candidate or proof review. `jam17-cost-review.json` binds exact source and pinned Lean primitive bytes. No compiler or timing was run here.

The legal mechanism really differs from R18's full UInt64 roundtrip. `normalCore` constructs two 24-bit mantissas, a 48-bit product, then a 31-bit jammed integer; its forward and reverse numerical casts are UInt32. The fixed UInt32 reverse primitive is a plain inline C conversion guarded by `0 ≤ a < 2^32`; it contains no external `isnan` call. The old UInt64 diagnostic actually retained a second conversion, subtraction, sign-mask merge and upper saturation. Those old observed instructions do not establish the new UInt32 lowering, but they identify a concrete cost to attempt to remove.

There is no source-level reason to remove the sticky bit: it is required for correctness. There is also no source-level reason to replace UInt32 by signed32 as a speculative shortcut: fixed signed32 reverse invokes external `lean_float32_isnan`. A forward signed conversion being one instruction would not establish that the complete signed roundtrip is cheaper.

For the planned exact diagnostic, inspect the true guard-success arm of `R19Jam17.diagnosticEntry`; for a public candidate, derive the actual `BENCH_PLAN` MUL callback from MainFull rather than assuming a lambda number. The probe keeps public MUL at B2 and cannot demonstrate the new public runtime. Require actual mantissa masks/product → low17 flag/shift/OR → UInt32 forward conversion → reverse conversion → high/shift/exponent packing on that arm. Distinguish the original fallback from accepted code when recording calls, allocations and spills. Include its guard and prologue/epilogue costs; a short isolated normalCore is insufficient.

The relevant falsifiers are: unsigned64-style compensation survived despite the type change; 0/2^32 clamp work survived and offsets the saved work; a boxed or external helper is called on the accepted path; multiple integer conversions or materialization were introduced by integration; or the complete D shows no gain. Some clamp work surviving is not by itself a failure—full path cost decides. Mantissa bounds in Lean proofs are erased; source-visible masks can help optimization but do not prove LLVM used them.

Only if actual optimized code exposes a missed 31-bit bound, a cheap next *hypothesis* is an explicit sign-bit-clearing mask on the already-proved `jamWord` result. It is semantically redundant on this product domain and might expose the bound earlier, but may be compiled away or add work. Do not create or queue that variant now. First collect the existing exact source's code, and make one symptom-specific decision. No new CI request follows from this review.
