# Common-exponent scale hoisting: PARK before implementation

Source/domain prerequisite23:57 UTC onward, no runtime/proof/CI. The distinct idea is to retain the original ordered Float32 fold, perform it on signed integer significands with a common positive power-of-two scale, then scale its final output once. It does not reassociate the fold or assume every product/prefix is exact. Exact power-of-two homogeneity under no-underflow/no-overflow would be the needed theorem. This differs from the earlier exact-lattice requirement but does not automatically save the native MAC work.

Paper sufficient domains screened, not tuned: SUM has a common exponent E24..200 among nonzero normal inputs, signed zeros allowed, length≤2^20. BMM has one such common exponent per A row/B column, their sum174..320, and inner length≤2^20. Unscaled significands are integer Float32; rounded products and ordered prefixes remain on an integer lattice. Crude one-bit headroom bounds unscaled SUM prefixes below2^45 and BMM prefixes below2^69. The chosen scales then keep nonzero values normal and finite. These are paper prerequisites; no FloatLib homogeneity/zero-sign theorem or full inductive bound was compiled. Final scale access and per-input sign handling remain real work.

Historical search found scalar exponent rescaling/R14, R17 scaled-U32 casts, R19 quantized exact-prefix certificates, and low-normal scalar rescaling, but no complete measured tensor fold with this particular common-exponent guard in the inspected records. This is a bounded history search, not a novelty claim. Existing R22 raw4/prepared4 results are not a pure input-conversion ablation and cannot measure the available scaling cost. That fraction remains UNKNOWN.

Relevant R20 `mathematical/SEMANTIC-REUSE.md` already preserves the counterexample A=[00800000,00800000], B=[33800000,3f800001]: scaling the completed dot by2 gives01000001, while scaling A first gives01000002. Normal inputs/endpoints alone are insufficient because an intermediate product underflows. It is outside the deliberately stronger sum-of-common-exponents174..320 domain above. The new paper argument still needs the full intermediate inductive bound; the old counterexample is not erased by renaming the scheme homogeneity.

Every SUM row and BMM row/column in the three already seen R15 plans was classified, stopping invalid summaries at the first counterexample. Full accepted summaries read all required elements. Recorded early-read totals are1748475,1071550,2059515 words. Third first attempt hit its2M read cap and produced no partial result; the separate v2 lifted only that cap to4M under the same18s ceiling and completed in6.75s. Original script and stop receipt are preserved. No correctness random tests were added.

There are **zero whole nonzero SUM cases**. Four whole nonzero BMM cases qualify, all n=1: run40168687803/sparse5 has m26,p3; run40169957447/ml2 has m6,p2 and sparse1 m46,p6; run40171180249/ml3 has m4,p1. Relative to shared preconverted inputs, removing at most n(m+p) input scale multiplies and adding mp final scale multiplies gives per-batch counts29→78,8→12,52→276,5→4 respectively, even before the summary scan, signed-coefficient preparation, output factor access and fallback. This is an operation-count comparison, not cycles. The first three cannot justify the route by multiply elimination; the last saves only one input scale multiply per batch and has no long accumulation.

Partial eligible rows/cells exist and are recorded, but large counts are concentrated in short lengths, zeros and n=1 matrices. SUM whole workload shares are9.24%,5.33%,6.91% of these old packages, before any eligibility or savings discount. Entire affected-cell ceilings18.53%,17.47%,22.12% substantially overcount partially eligible work and are **not** achievable savings. No input-conversion timing fraction or10% removable-work case is established. Mixed row/column handling would require more metadata/preparation and a separate cost argument; it is not silently included.

Decision: PARK the common-exponent tensor scheme; do not create a generic rescaling proof or candidate. Keep the finite domain receipts as negative prerequisite evidence. A wider exponent-span scheme would add variable shifts/wider casts, summary cost and output scaling while leaving native MACs unchanged; it is not funded by this result. Current B's scalar native-product-scale experiment remains separate and may have a substantially larger scalar-operation cost share.
